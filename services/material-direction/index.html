All services Material direction · a specialist layer

A material palette that holds up to a question.

For projects with a designer already in place. We come in as the material layer: sourcing, vetting, sample assembly, and claims you can stand behind in a meeting.

ForDesign teams who want their materials chosen by people who specify them every week and have to live with how they age.

Most palettes fall apart in the second meeting. Someone asks where the oak is from, or what the acoustic felt is actually made of, and the answer is a shrug.

Material direction exists for the project that already has good bones. You have an architect, or an interior designer, and the plan is sound. What is thin is the material conviction underneath it. The mood board looks lovely and cannot survive a procurement review.

We sit beside your design team as a specialist layer. We source the things the room is asking for, vet them for what they are made of and how they behave, build physical sample sets you can hold under the actual light of the space, and write the claims down so they can be defended. When the client, the contractor, or the sustainability assessor asks a hard question, the answer is already on the page.

We work in materials every week, on our own restructure projects. So we recommend what we have lived with, not what photographs well in a catalogue.

How the layer works

Roughly six to ten weeks alongside your design team, depending on the size of the palette and how much of it has to be sourced from scratch.

01

We read the palette you have

We start with your drawings, your specification, and whatever mood work exists. We mark what is decided, what is a placeholder, and what is a claim waiting to be tested. You get an honest first pass: which selections will hold, and which will not survive a question about provenance, fire rating, or off-gassing.

02

We source against the room, not the catalogue

We go to the makers. FSC-certified oak and walnut from named mills, lime plaster and clay paints with full ingredient disclosure, wool and jute textiles with no PFAS finish, cork, linoleum on a jute backing, terrazzo with recycled aggregate, reclaimed stone. We check lead times, minimum orders, and whether the maker can actually deliver at your quantity before we ever show it to you.

03

We assemble samples for the actual light

We build physical sample sets and view them in the space at the times of day the room is used, because oak reads orange at 4pm and grey at 9am and a screen will lie to you about both. We test the soft materials for sound, the hard ones for wear, and we leave the difficult adjacencies (the join where timber meets felt meets stone) sitting together for a week before anyone signs off.

04

We write claims you can defend

Every material in the final palette comes with its receipt: certification, recycled content, VOC figures, end-of-life route, and the maker behind it. Where a claim cannot be proven, we say so plainly rather than dress it up. The pack is built to feed straight into a WELL or BREEAM submission, and to be read aloud in a budget meeting without a single gap.

Candidate timber and fibre samples, read in the space at the hours the room is used.

Why we test under the real light, every time

A material decision made on a screen is a guess. Daylight in a working office moves through a wide swing of colour temperature across the day, and most acoustic and surface finishes are chosen, signed off, and installed without ever being seen in it. That is how a room ends up looking nothing like the render.

We bring the candidate palette into the space and read it at the hours the room is used. Warm low sun raises the red in timber and clay. An overcast morning flattens everything toward grey and shows up the cheapness in a poor laminate. We also measure: soft, fibrous surfaces are chosen to pull the room toward a calm 35 to 40 dB at the desk, and we check that the materials carrying that load are doing it honestly, not just looking soft.

The same discipline runs through the green claims. We will not put a recycled-content figure on a page we cannot trace to a document. A palette that holds up to a question is one where every answer was found before the question was asked.

Included · what you get

What we hand the design team

A vetted material schedule

Every selection named to the specific product, finish, and maker, with substitutes ranked behind each one in case of a lead-time problem. No generic line items that a contractor can quietly downgrade.

Physical sample boxes

Two assembled sets: one for the studio, one to live on site under real light. Soft and hard materials shown together in the adjacencies they will actually meet, not as isolated swatches on a card.

A claims dossier

One page per material: certification numbers, recycled and bio-based content, VOC and formaldehyde figures, fire classification, and the documented end-of-life route. Written to survive a procurement audit and to slot into a green-building submission.

A sourcing map

Where each material comes from, the named supplier, minimum order, lead time, and an honest note on price volatility. So the people ordering know exactly what they are committing to.

Notes on ageing and care

How each surface behaves over five years: what patinas, what scratches, what needs re-oiling, and what should never see a standard cleaning product. The room stays good because someone wrote down how to keep it good.

Questions · before you ask
We already have an architect. Where do you sit?

Behind them, on materials only. We do not touch the plan, the layout, or the design intent. We take the palette your designer is working toward and make it real and defensible: sourcing, sampling, vetting, and the claims pack. Your design team stays in charge of the room.

What makes a sustainability claim defensible?

A document behind it. We only put a figure on the page if we can trace it to a certificate, a manufacturer declaration, or an EPD. FSC chain-of-custody for timber, ingredient disclosure for paints and plasters, measured VOC and formaldehyde figures, and a real end-of-life route. Where the proof does not exist, we say the claim cannot be made rather than soften it. That is what survives an assessor.

Can this feed a WELL or BREEAM submission?

Yes, and it is built to. The claims dossier is structured around the material and indoor-air criteria those frameworks assess: low-emission finishes, responsibly sourced timber, recycled content, and transparency of ingredients. We assemble the evidence in the form your sustainability consultant needs, so the material side of the submission is largely done.

How long does the engagement take?

Usually six to ten weeks alongside your design team. The variable is how much of the palette has to be sourced from scratch versus tightened from selections you already have. Sample assembly and the week we leave difficult adjacencies sitting together under real light are fixed parts of every engagement and we do not compress them.
