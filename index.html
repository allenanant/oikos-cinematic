IA Letter

What we mean
when we say oikos.

From the desk of
Angelina
Founder · Lodi Road, Delhi
Written one Sunday in March, when the jasmine on the balcony had just opened.

Oikos is the Greek word for the small ecosystem of a home — the household, the family of objects and people inside it, the quiet weather of the rooms. Centuries later, the same root grew into ecology and economy. Both words still ask the same question, in their own way: how do we live well together, in a finite place?

I started Oikos because most of what is sold as “sustainable” feels neither warm nor considered. It comes in plastic, it ships in three boxes, it sits on the counter unused. I wanted a practice that did the opposite. Spaces that take their cues from the light you actually have. Gifts that are still in use a year later. A workplace concierge that replaces the things your office quietly runs on — tissues, soap, scent, refills — before anyone has to ask, and from people who make them well.

None of it is rigid. A room is permitted to be imperfect. A paper-tied parcel is permitted to look hand-made. The whole point is that beauty and care should not be at war with the planet, or with the people doing the work.

A room should exhale a little when you walk in. So should the things you put inside it.

If any of that resonates — for a home, a studio, a hotel room, or the office your team comes back to every Monday — I would love to hear from you. The practice runs slowly, on conversations rather than briefs.

— AngelinaFounder, Oikos

IIIThe Practice

Three ways
we bring oikos in.

01
Of rooms

Biophilic interiors

Rooms designed to take their cues from the light you actually have, the way a body wants to move through a space, and the planting you can keep alive next March. Materials that age well. Layouts that have somewhere to put a hand.

Each project begins with a long walk through the space, no notebook, no brief. The drawings come later.

“We don't decorate rooms. We listen to them, and then we add a little.”

Suited for

Studios, café & retail interiors, boutique hotel suites, corporate offices that want to feel less like corporate offices.

Begin a room
02
Of gestures

Conscious gifting

Hampers, festival gifts, wedding favours and quiet thank-yous, assembled by hand. Sourced from small makers and built around things people keep, not consume in a week.

Every parcel is tied in twine and finished with a wax seal. The wrapping itself is part of the gift.

“A small, slow ritual on both ends — the making, and the opening.”

Suited for

Personal gifting, wedding favours, client appreciation, employee onboarding, festive hampers.

Curate a parcel
03
Of upkeep

A workplace concierge

A standing service for the things your workplace quietly runs on — hand soap, tissues, room scents, pantry refills, washroom essentials, the seasonal touches that make a room feel cared for.

Sourced from sustainable brands. Delivered on a schedule that matches your usage. Replenished before anyone has to ask.

“One conversation at the start. Ongoing care after that.”

Suited for

Offices, co-working spaces, boutique hotels, clinics, studios.
