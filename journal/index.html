No. 04 · Feature 02 Field notes, March

A warehouse that refused to dry out.

We took on the Sultanpur warehouse in the wettest week of August. The shed had been sitting empty since 1998, and the air inside held the dense, slow humidity of an old library. The brief from the client was simple and almost rude. Do not turn this into another open-plan office. Do not put glass meeting rooms in it. Do not let it dry out.

For weeks the question of how a building stays damp without staying mouldy was the only thing on the studio whiteboard. Lime plaster. Old brick. Hand-pressed terracotta. Vertical planting walls fed from a slow drip we hid in the truss. None of it was decorative. All of it was working.

The acoustician we trust came in twice, once before and once after. The reverb dropped eleven decibels. The dry-air complaints, which the client's team had been logging in a private Slack channel since their last office, dropped to zero in the second month. We did not fix the room with software. We fixed it with mass, weave and water.

Two seasons in, the warehouse has held. The planting wall is on its third quiet pruning. The lime ceiling smells faintly of the rain. Nobody on the team has asked for a humidifier. The room, you might say, has remembered what it used to be.
